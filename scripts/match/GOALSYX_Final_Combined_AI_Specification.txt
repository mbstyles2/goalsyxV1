GOALSYX — FINAL FORMATION-BASED ATTACKING AND DEFENSIVE AI SPECIFICATION
Godot 3 | Football Manager-Inspired Tactics + FIFA/PES-Inspired Responsiveness

============================================================
1. OBJECTIVE
============================================================

Act as an experienced Godot 3 gameplay AI programmer and football-simulation systems designer. Design and implement an intelligent attacking and defensive AI system for GOALSYX, a 2D six-a-side football game.

Combine:

- Football Manager-inspired intelligence: tactical positioning, formation discipline, player roles, space management, coordinated team movement, defensive structure, and tactical identity.
- FIFA/PES-inspired responsiveness: responsive movement, ball-carrier pressure, tackles, interceptions, quick passing, dribbling, shooting decisions, and immediate reactions to changing situations.

Every team must have its own formation, tactic, player roles, and AI configuration. Both teams must operate independently and react to one another.

The AI must not simply move every player toward the ball. It must understand where each player should move, why that position matters, and how the movement supports the team's current tactical plan.

The final gameplay should feel tactically organised while remaining responsive, readable, efficient, and enjoyable in an arcade-style football game.

B. NEW AttackAI.gd — ATTACKING BEHAVIOUR

Responsibilities:

- Encourage passing triangles, useful support angles, and more than one option for the ball carrier.
- Stop off-ball players from clustering around the ball or occupying the same space.
- Position off-ball players to provide width, support the ball carrier, run into open space, attack space behind defenders, or retain defensive cover according to their roles.
- Make shooting decisions based on distance, angle, pressure, goalkeeper position if available, and whether a better passing option exists.
- Make dribbling decisions based on pressure, space, player attributes if present, and whether passing or shooting is preferable.
- Respect formation and role responsibilities; not every player should act like a striker.
- Support fast counterattacks when the selected tactic calls for them.
- Use existing movement and action functions. Do not create another movement controller or ball-control system.

C. NEW DefendAI.gd — DEFENSIVE BEHAVIOUR

Responsibilities:
- Organise the team when the opposition has possession.
- Position players relative to the ball, the goal they defend, formation, role, teammates, opponents, and immediate threats.
- Support marking, covering, pressing, tackling, interceptions, blocking passing lanes, tracking runners, recovery runs, and defensive-line behaviour where supported by the existing game.
- Decide which player should pressure the ball carrier and which players should cover passing options, dangerous spaces, and potential runners.
- Avoid having every defender chase the ball. Coordinate pressure, cover, and balance.
- Protect central areas when appropriate while responding to wide threats.
- Maintain sensible spacing and avoid unnecessary clustering or crossing paths.
- Adjust pressure and recovery behaviour according to the selected tactic and match state.
- Reuse existing goalkeeper logic if it exists. Do not create GoalkeeperAI.gd unless the existing project structure specifically requires a new goalkeeper module and the need is established after inspection.
- Use existing movement, tackling, and ball-interaction functions rather than duplicating them.

D. NEW PlayerDecisionAI.gd — PLAYER-LEVEL DECISION MAKING

Responsibilities:
- Act as the decision layer for each AI-controlled player.
- Observe relevant match information: ball position and velocity if available, possession, player/team positions, opponents, open space, distance to goal, player role, available attributes, formation, tactic, and match state.
- Decide what the player should do next.
- Route the selected behaviour to AttackAI.gd or DefendAI.gd as appropriate.
- Distinguish between attacking, defending, transition to attack, and transition to defence.
- Prevent conflicting actions, constant decision switching, unnecessary movement, and multiple players chasing the ball.
- Use readable decision rules and priorities first. Add weighted scoring, cooldowns, or more advanced decision selection only where they materially improve behaviour.
- Reassess decisions when possession, ball position, pressure, danger, or tactical conditions change.
- Separate decision selection from execution:
  * PlayerDecisionAI.gd decides WHAT the player should do.
  * AttackAI.gd or DefendAI.gd determines the relevant behaviour and how to execute it through existing systems.

5. SHARED AI DECISION LOOP
============================================================

Every AI-controlled player should follow this conceptual loop:

1. OBSERVE: Identify the ball, teammates, opponents, goals, open spaces, passing lanes, and immediate threats.
2. UNDERSTAND: Determine possession and whether the team is attacking, defending, transitioning to attack, or transitioning to defence.
3. CONSULT: Read the team's formation, tactic, and the player's role.
4. EVALUATE: Assess available actions, space, pressure, danger, support, and counterattack risk.
5. DECIDE: Select one appropriate action with a clear priority.
6. EXECUTE: Move toward a sensible target or perform the selected action through existing systems.
7. REASSESS: Update the decision when meaningful conditions change.

Use sensible decision intervals, action priorities, target-position smoothing, or cooldowns where useful to prevent jitter and constant decision switching. Movement should still feel responsive. Avoid long decision delays that make players feel unresponsive.

Do not recalculate expensive decisions unnecessarily every frame if a small decision interval or event-driven update is suitable. Urgent situations—such as a shot, a dangerous ball near goal, a loose ball, or a possession change—must still trigger prompt reactions.

6. THE FOUR TACTICAL STYLES
============================================================

All four tactics must influence both attacking and defensive behaviour. They must not be implemented merely as labels or changes to player speed.

A. COUNTERATTACK

Attacking:
- Transition forward quickly after winning possession.
- Look for forward passes, through balls, and runners attacking open space.
- Send suitable attackers forward while keeping enough support and protection behind the ball.
- Prefer exploiting an unbalanced defence over repeatedly passing sideways.
- Take early shots or deliver crosses/cutbacks when the opportunity is good.
- Use direct play when it creates a real advantage, not as an unconditional rule.

Defending:
- Maintain a compact, organised shape rather than pressing constantly.
- Protect central areas and dangerous passing lanes.
- Let the opponent advance into less dangerous areas before committing to a challenge when appropriate.
- Keep players positioned to launch a counterattack after winning possession.
- Recover quickly if the first defensive line is bypassed.

Identity: Absorb pressure, win possession, and attack the space left behind.

B. BALANCED

Attacking:
- Mix short passes, forward passes, dribbling, and supporting runs.
- Maintain width and provide nearby passing options.
- Advance when space is available without sending too many players forward.
- Choose between patient build-up and quick attacks according to the situation.

Defending:
- Maintain a compact shape with sensible spacing.
- Press when the opportunity is good and recover when the press is beaten.
- Mark dangerous opponents and protect central areas.
- Balance pressure on the ball with cover and recovery runs.

Identity: Adapt to the match without an extreme attacking or defensive commitment.

C. RETAIN POSSESSION

Attacking:
- Prefer safe short and medium-distance passes when useful.
- Create passing triangles and multiple options around the ball carrier.
- Move intelligently to provide support and passing angles.
- Recycle possession when forward progress is blocked.
- Use width to stretch the defence and move forward when a clear opening appears.
- Avoid unnecessary long balls, low-probability passes, and speculative shots.
- Do not keep possession pointlessly when a clear high-quality chance is available.

Defending:
- Keep the team compact enough to prevent dangerous counterattacks.
- Press intelligently after losing possession, especially near the ball.
- Keep players positioned to recover loose balls and protect central spaces.
- Avoid reckless challenges that leave gaps.
- After regaining possession, secure the ball when appropriate rather than forcing an immediate forward attack.

Identity: Control the game through passing, movement, spacing, and intelligent possession recovery.

D. GEGENPRESS

Attacking:
- Use forward movement, quick combinations, and supporting runners.
- Keep nearby teammates close enough to support the ball carrier and contest loose balls.
- Use forward passes when the defence is exposed.
- Position players to support an immediate press if possession is lost.
- Balance attacking numbers with protection against counterattacks.

Defending and transition:
- Apply immediate coordinated pressure after losing possession.
- Nearby players pressure the ball or close passing options; other players block escape routes and cover dangerous spaces.
- Players farther from the ball retain defensive cover instead of blindly joining the press.
- Try to regain possession quickly, then attack while the opponent is disorganised.
- If the press is bypassed, recover into the defensive formation instead of chasing indefinitely.

Identity: Win the ball back quickly through coordinated pressure and exploit the opponent's disorganisation.

7. FORMATIONS AND THEIR TACTICAL IDENTITIES
============================================================

All four tactics must work with all six formation identities. Do not implement 24 unrelated AI systems. Use a shared decision engine and configurable tactical parameters.

IMPORTANT: The following are 11-a-side formation descriptions used to define tactical identities. Translate them into valid six-a-side formations for GOALSYX, taking the actual number of field players and goalkeeper into account. Do not create extra players. Document the six-a-side role mapping for each formation and ensure every player has a valid position and responsibility.

A. 4-3-3

Traditional identity: Four defenders, three midfielders, three forwards.

- Attacking: Wingers stretch the pitch, the striker threatens the defensive line, and midfielders create passing options, make forward runs, or control the centre. Fullbacks overlap when appropriate.
- Defensive: The front three can press the opponent's build-up. Midfielders protect central passing lanes while the back line remains coordinated.
- Counterattack: Wingers and striker attack open space immediately; midfielders release forward passes while remaining players protect against counters.
- Balanced: Wingers, midfielders, and wide defenders advance selectively while midfield retains cover.
- Retain Possession: Midfielders form passing triangles, wide players provide width, and wide defenders support circulation without both abandoning defensive cover.
- Gegenpress: The front attackers and nearest midfielders pressure the ball; remaining players cover central spaces and forward runs.

B. 4-2-3-1

Traditional identity: Four defenders, two deeper midfielders, three attacking midfielders, and one striker.

- Attacking: The central attacking midfielder links midfield to attack. Wide attackers stretch or cut inside, while the striker occupies defenders. Wide defenders support selectively.
- Defensive: Deeper midfielders protect the centre and screen passes. Attacking midfielders support pressing and track dangerous runners.
- Counterattack: The central attacking player releases the striker or wide attackers quickly. Deeper midfielders provide initial defensive protection.
- Balanced: The central attacking player connects the lines while deeper midfielders balance forward movement and cover.
- Retain Possession: Deeper midfielders offer safe passing outlets; the attacking midfielder finds pockets of space and wide players provide passing angles.
- Gegenpress: The forward line pressures the opponent's first pass while deeper midfielders cover central exits.

C. 4-4-2

Traditional identity: Four defenders, four midfielders, and two strikers.

- Attacking: Wide midfielders provide width and crosses. Two forwards coordinate movement; one may run behind while the other supports or links play.
- Defensive: Midfielders maintain a compact line, protect flanks and centre, and work with forwards to apply pressure.
- Counterattack: Forwards provide immediate targets. Wide players advance quickly and central midfielders support without exposing the defence.
- Balanced: Two compact lines support wide attacks, forward combinations, and selective runs.
- Retain Possession: Central midfielders offer short passing options, wide players maintain width, and a forward can drop deeper to link play.
- Gegenpress: Forwards pressure defenders, wide players close passing lanes, and central midfielders contest loose balls while deeper players retain cover.

D. 3-5-2

Traditional identity: Three centre-backs, five midfielders including wingbacks, and two strikers.

- Attacking: Wingbacks provide width and crosses. Central midfielders control the centre while forwards combine and occupy defenders.
- Defensive: Three central defenders protect the middle. Wide players recover to defend the flanks, and midfielders block dangerous central passes.
- Counterattack: Forwards offer immediate outlets. Wide players advance when safe while enough players protect against turnovers.
- Balanced: Wide players advance selectively, midfielders share responsibilities, and the back line provides stability.
- Retain Possession: Midfielders create passing triangles; wide players maintain width and forwards provide outlets without forcing poor passes.
- Gegenpress: Nearby midfielders and forwards pressure the ball; wide players close wide exits and deeper defenders cover space behind the press.

E. 4-5-1

Traditional identity: Four defenders, five midfielders, and one striker.

- Attacking: Midfielders support the lone forward, wide players provide width, and central midfielders make selective forward runs. Avoid leaving the forward isolated.
- Defensive: Midfielders form a compact barrier in front of the back line. The forward presses or screens useful passes.
- Counterattack: The forward provides a target while wide players sprint into space. Central midfielders support the break and retain cover.
- Balanced: The midfield provides defensive security while selected players advance to support attacks.
- Retain Possession: Midfielders circulate the ball and create passing triangles; they rotate to support the lone forward.
- Gegenpress: Nearby midfielders pressure the ball while the rest retain central compactness. The forward blocks or pressures a useful escape pass.

F. 3-4-3

Traditional identity: Three centre-backs, four midfielders including wide players/wingbacks, and three forwards.

- Attacking: The front three stretch the defence, make runs behind the defensive line, and combine in wide and central areas. Wide midfielders provide width when forwards move inside.
- Defensive: Three central defenders protect the middle while wide players recover to cover the flanks. The front three can press opposing defenders.
- Counterattack: The front three attack open space immediately, supported by early passes from midfield. Deeper players protect against turnovers.
- Balanced: The front three create threats while midfielders advance selectively and deeper players retain cover.
- Retain Possession: Forwards stretch the opposition, midfielders provide passing options, and wide players maintain width. Recycle the ball when forward lanes close.
- Gegenpress: The front attackers and nearest midfielders pressure the ball and block passing lanes; others cover central and wide threats.

FORMATION AND TACTIC INTERACTION

Do not hard-code every formation/tactic combination separately. For each player, calculate behaviour and a target position using:
- Formation and current positional role.
- Selected tactical style.
- Current possession and transition state.
- Ball location and direction of attack.
- Teammate and opponent positions.
- Available space and passing lanes.
- Player attributes and stamina if already supported.
- Current counterattack risk.
- Current defensive and attacking balance.

Blend the player's formation-based home position with a situation-dependent target. For example, a wide defender may advance to support possession, but should remain more conservative if another wide defender is already forward and the opponent has a counterattacking threat.

Tactics should modify parameters such as pressing intensity, forward movement, passing risk, support distance, defensive depth, transition speed, and the number of players committed to attacks.

10. MATCH STATES AND TRANSITIONS
============================================================

Use or extend the existing match-state and possession logic to represent:

- ATTACKING: This team has possession and is trying to create a scoring opportunity.
- DEFENDING: The opponent has possession and this team is protecting its goal.
- TRANSITION_TO_ATTACK: This team has just won possession and must choose between a quick attack and controlled possession.
- TRANSITION_TO_DEFENCE: This team has just lost possession and must choose between immediate pressure and recovery into shape.

When possession changes, both teams must promptly update their behaviour.

Winning possession:
- Choose attacking behaviour based on tactic, formation, available space, pressure, and nearby support.
- Counterattack should prioritise forward space when the opportunity exists.
- Retain Possession should secure the ball when appropriate.
- Gegenpress should exploit a regain while the opposition is disorganised.

Losing possession:
- Gegenpress should initiate coordinated counterpressure.
- Counterattack should recover compactly and protect dangerous areas.
- Balanced should use situational pressure and recovery.
- Retain Possession should press intelligently near the ball while protecting against counters.

Do not teleport all players instantly into formation. Players should move naturally toward new target positions, preserve momentum where sensible, and prioritise urgent threats. Avoid rapid switching between contradictory actions due to minor fluctuations in possession or player positions.

15. FINAL DESIGN PRINCIPLE
============================================================

GOALSYX should combine Football Manager-style tactical team intelligence with FIFA/PES-style responsive gameplay.

Formation determines the team's base shape. Tactic determines the team's overall behaviour. Role determines individual responsibilities. The match situation determines the immediate action.

The existing tactic.gd remains the tactics manager. PlayerDecisionAI.gd decides what each player should do. AttackAI.gd and DefendAI.gd handle the appropriate attacking or defensive behaviour. Existing movement, ball, possession, formation, role, and goalkeeper systems should be reused wherever possible.

Use a shared, configurable decision system—not 24 unrelated formation/tactic implementations. Preserve the agreed architecture, adapt the formation identities to GOALSYX's actual six-a-side player count, inspect the code before modifying it, and build and test the AI in manageable stages.
